\section{Discussion}
The central finding is that ADC, despite collapsing the multi-component signal to a single scalar, is a near-optimal spectral discriminant for tumor detection: across zones and estimators it ranks ROIs almost identically to the optimal eight-component classifier (Figure~\ref{fig:adc_discriminant}).
Rather than an impoverished summary of a richer signal, ADC captures the dominant axis of spectral variation relevant to detection---the spectral decomposition does not obsolete ADC, it explains it.
This is consistent with recent works in which advanced diffusion models matched ADC for prostate cancer detection \citep{langkilde2018evaluation, he2025improved}.

\subsection{Why ADC works}
The mechanism is visible in one view (Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity}): both readouts place nearly all of their weight on the same two diffusivity bins, with the same signs.
ADC and the spectral discriminant are both, to first order, linear functionals of the same eight-bin spectrum, and across the cohort the tumor-versus-normal variation is concentrated in two well-identified compartments---restricted diffusion ($D = 0.25\;\umsmm$) rising as free water ($D = 3.0\;\umsmm$) falls---so the spectra vary along an essentially one-dimensional axis. Two linear readouts projecting onto that single axis must rank ROIs almost identically, which is what we observe ($|r| > 0.97$).
The ADC sensitivity vector confirms the alignment: $\partial\text{ADC}/\partial R_j$ is largest in magnitude and of opposite sign on those two bins ($-1.74$ at $D = 0.25$ and $+0.83$ at $D = 3.0\;\umsmm$, at the mean tumor spectrum), and is anti-correlated with the discriminant coefficients across bins ($r = -0.79$ PZ, $-0.88$ TZ)---the expected sign, since tumor corresponds to \emph{low} ADC, so the tumor-pointing sensitivity is $-\partial\text{ADC}/\partial\Rvec$.
The agreement is carried by the restricted and free-water bins, which in both zones are the only ones whose standardized coefficients are both large and bootstrap-separated from zero; two intermediate bins reach nominal significance in the transition zone ($D = 0.75$ and $1.0\;\umsmm$) but with coefficients three to eight times smaller.

This does not mean ADC ignores the intermediate compartments.
ADC remains appreciably sensitive there, but discriminative power is sensitivity \emph{times} contrast: the intermediate fractions carry little tumor--normal contrast beyond the restricted and free-water pair and are poorly identified (Figure~\ref{fig:fisher}), so ADC's sensitivity to them is spent on a low-contrast, noisy direction that moves neither score's ranking. They are therefore redundant rather than uninformative---each co-varies with the dominant axis and discriminates on its own, yet adds nothing once the restricted and free-water compartments are present (Table~\ref{tab:auc}).

This is why resolving more diffusion compartments \citep{panagiotaki2014microstructural, conlin2021improved} need not improve tumor detection over ADC in an acquisition such as ours: the extra degrees of freedom point, in spectrum space, nearly orthogonally to the direction along which tumor and normal tissue differ. Where richer models do gain on detection, the gain traces to contrast this acquisition lacks: hybrid multidimensional MRI separates tumor from normal almost perfectly, but by adding an echo-time dimension, not finer diffusion compartments \citep{chatterjee2018hybrid}.
That the observable variation is one-dimensional is not in tension with the ill-conditioning of the design---the two compound. The singular values of $\Umat$ span five orders of magnitude, so at the cohort-median noise level only about five of the eight directions in spectrum space are determined to better than $10\%$ relative precision, and of those only one carries appreciable tumor--normal contrast. Ill-conditioning caps how many spectral directions an extended-b acquisition can resolve; biology decides how many are worth resolving.

\subsection{Grade-dependent spectral shifts}
% NOTE (2026-07-26): kept descriptive. The spectrum makes grade-associated biology visible, but we claim no per-ROI grading advantage over ADC and report no grading AUC (small n). The formal discriminative-power framework was removed per Stephan's review; it is deferred to a follow-up paper.
While detection collapses onto the restricted-cellular and free-water bins, any signal beyond ADC would have to live in the intermediate bins, where ADC is least sensitive---and this is plausibly where the decomposition adds value (Figure~\ref{fig:spectrum_ggg}).
The free-water bin ($D = 3.0\;\umsmm$, a proxy for intact gland lumen) collapses as soon as tumor is present ($0.39$ normal to $0.21$ at GGG\,$=\,1$) and then changes little ($0.16$ at GGG\,$\geq 3$): it separates tumor from normal, not grade from grade.
By contrast the restricted-cellular bin ($D = 0.25\;\umsmm$) rises monotonically with grade ($0.07$ normal to $0.31$ at GGG\,$\geq 3$), and the glandular-epithelial bin ($D = 2.0\;\umsmm$) is preserved in low-grade tumor and depleted at higher grades ($0.26$ normal, $0.28$ at GGG\,$=\,1$, $0.17$ at GGG\,$\geq 3$).

Because the grade groups are too small to subdivide, this comparison pools the two zones, whose normal baselines differ (Results); the graded tumors are predominantly peripheral-zone (21 of 29) while the normal baseline is evenly split. Two checks indicate that pooling does not manufacture the pattern.
First, pooling is conservative: peripheral-zone normal tissue carries more free water ($0.48$ vs.\ $0.30$) and less restricted mass ($0.06$ vs.\ $0.09$) than transition-zone normal tissue, so a zone-matched baseline for the PZ-dominated tumor groups would show a \emph{larger} free-water collapse and a \emph{larger} restricted rise.
Second, the pattern survives restriction to the peripheral zone alone ($n = 21$): the restricted bin still rises across GGG\,$=\,1$, $2$ and $\geq 3$ ($0.16 \to 0.17 \to 0.28$, against $0.06$ in normal tissue) and the glandular-epithelial bin still falls ($0.29 \to 0.25 \to 0.17$), its within-zone rank correlation with grade remaining nominally significant (Spearman $\rho = -0.44$, $P = 0.045$).

This pattern follows the cellular reorganization of prostate adenocarcinoma---loss of patent lumina, glandular effacement, and rising cell-packing density---and is consistent with histological work relating epithelium, stroma, and lumen volumes to Gleason pattern and to ADC \citep{chatterjee2015changes}, volumes that multidimensional MRI recovers in vivo \citep{chatterjee2022validation}, and with reports that advanced diffusion models improve grade differentiation where ADC plateaus \citep{johnston2019verdict, he2025improved}.
We present this as a candidate interpretation the decomposition makes observable, not a validated finding: the grade groups are small ($n = 8$--$9$) and unbalanced by zone---higher-grade lesions are relatively more often transition-zone---so no grading AUC or between-method comparison is claimed.

\subsection{What the Bayesian posterior contributes}
At tuned regularization the MAP point estimate and the NUTS posterior mean agreed closely on the well-identified components and gave nearly interchangeable discriminant scores ($r = 0.99$ PZ, $0.97$ TZ), so closed-form MAP is sufficient for classification.
Their divergence on the intermediate components reflects the ill-posedness rather than a deficiency of either method: those fractions are genuinely underdetermined by fifteen b-values, even at the low noise of our endorectal-coil acquisition, and the sampler reproduces the same smearing on simulated ground truth (Figure~\ref{fig:validation}). The posterior makes the resulting identifiability gradient explicit---the three structurally distinct bins ($D = 0.25$, $3.0$, $20\;\umsmm$) are well determined (CV $0.20$--$0.36$), the most collinear intermediate bins ($D = 0.5$--$1.0\;\umsmm$) essentially unconstrained (CV $> 0.8$).
What makes the well-constrained components trustworthy is the two-step tightening of the attainable estimation error in Figure~\ref{fig:fisher}b, which moves this classically ill-conditioned separation \citep{jalnefjord2019optimization} into a far better-posed regime.
The posterior's value is therefore upstream---establishing which compartments can be believed---rather than in the tumor-versus-normal decision itself.

Consistent with this, propagating the full posterior through the classifier added no diagnostic value beyond the point estimate (Figure~\ref{fig:uncertainty}): detection rides on the restricted and free-water compartments, leaving little estimation uncertainty in the discriminant direction to propagate, and supplying the per-component posterior standard deviations as additional features left detection AUC unchanged.
The credible intervals on $P(\mathrm{tumor})$ are on average $2.4\times$ wider for misclassified ROIs, but this is a geometric property of the logistic link---its slope is largest at the decision boundary---not a contribution of the spectral posterior: in logit space the difference is no longer significant.
Because errors concentrate near the boundary, distance from the boundary does flag the least reliable predictions, a useful triage signal---but that is a standard property of the logistic model, available from the point estimate alone and shared by the ADC- and spectrum-based classifiers.

\subsection{Pixel-wise feasibility}
The framework extends to the voxel level (Figure~\ref{fig:pixelwise}).
On a single peripheral-zone slice the restricted-diffusion ($D = 0.25\;\umsmm$) map reproduced the ADC pattern, the free-water ($D = 3.0\;\umsmm$) map was its negative, and the per-voxel discriminant anti-correlated with ADC ($r = -0.96$); the per-voxel uncertainty map showed the eight-bin detector $1.8\times$ more uncertain than the two-bin one, because it weights the unidentifiable intermediate compartments.
It also exposes a calibration caveat: the much lower SNR of a single voxel inflates the restricted fraction, biasing the ROI-trained detector toward tumor, which is why all quantitative results are reported at the ROI level. The maps are exploratory---the uncertainty does not yet improve detection, but it indicates which compartments are locally reliable.
Three-direction acquisition in another patient cohort confirmed that the decomposition is direction-independent at the diagnostically dominant restricted bin (per-direction coefficient of variation $\approx 9\%$; Figure~\ref{fig:directions}), supporting the use of trace-averaged signal.

\subsection{Limitations and future directions}
First, the tumor ROIs were placed on multiparametric MRI, including DWI and ADC maps, introducing a potential circularity: lesions conspicuous on ADC may be overrepresented \citep{langkilde2018evaluation}, which could inflate ADC's apparent performance relative to spectral features. Compounding this, the detection-positive ROIs are radiological targets rather than biopsy-confirmed cancers---11 of the 40 were not confirmed malignant---so the detection contrast is partly against the radiologist's own ADC-informed reading; only the Gleason grade analysis, derived from biopsy pathology, is free of this circularity.
Second, the equivalence is specific to ROI-level detection of delineated lesions; for patient-level, delineation-free detection, compartment-isolating biomarkers such as restriction-spectrum imaging outperform whole-gland minimum ADC \citep{domingo2025restriction}---a different task.
Third, the graded sample ($n = 29$) is too small for a reliable between-method comparison of grading performance.
Fourth, the diffusivity grid is fixed; a finer or free-form grid might reveal more structure at the cost of greater ill-posedness.
Fifth, a single echo-time and diffusion-time configuration was analyzed; the shorter timings enabled by recent gradient hardware \citep{zhu2024human, elsaid2026nonlinear} could change both the SNR and the relative weight of the visible compartments.
Finally, the pixel-wise result rests on a single patient and requires larger studies with histopathological correlation.

The framework applies to other organs in which multi-b DWI is acquired, and the Fisher-information analysis could guide joint optimization of b-value protocols and diffusivity grids.
Integration with quantitative T\textsubscript{2} mapping, which independently resolves luminal water \citep{sabouri2017luminal}, could yield multiparametric spectral biomarkers that separate the grading axis from the detection axis characterized here.
